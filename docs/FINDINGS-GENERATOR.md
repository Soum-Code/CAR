# Does the local/global gap survive a stronger generator?

Every rate in this project came from Math-Shepherd, i.e. from **Mistral-7B-SFT**
at ~45% on GSM8K. The standing objection to C1 — that most globally-wrong steps
are arithmetically perfect — is that this is a property of one weak 2023 model,
and a better generator would close the gap by itself.

It does not. **The gap gets wider.**

Reproduce with:

```bash
python scripts/exp_generator_transfer.py
```

Corpus: `runs/generated_qwen25_7b.jsonl`, 500 GSM8K **test** problems solved by
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 8,292 rollouts, Kaggle T4×2, 7h40m. Generated by
`scripts/gpu_generator_transfer.py`; written in Math-Shepherd's own `label`
format so the same analysis code reads both corpora.

---

## The result

Within wrong-answer solutions, one definition applied to both corpora:

| | Mistral-7B-SFT | Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct |
|---|---|---|
| GSM8K accuracy | ~45% (reported) | **80.0%** (measured here) |
| local error | 0.1708 | **0.0813** |
| global error | 0.7106 | 0.5772 |
| **C1 — globally-wrong steps that are locally valid** | **0.7848** | **0.9040** |
| n globally-wrong checkable steps | 46,555 | 125 |
| 95% CI on C1 (Wilson) | [0.781, 0.789] | [0.840, 0.944] |

The intervals do not overlap.

The mechanism is straightforward once stated: **the stronger model halves its
arithmetic slips** (0.171 → 0.081) without halving its inherited corruption, so
a larger share of what remains is the kind no calculator can see.

> A deterministic verifier becomes *less* useful as the generator improves.

That is a sharper claim than the thesis previously made, and it is the opposite
of what the objection predicted.

## The notation problem, which nearly produced a wrong answer

Math-Shepherd's local check reads GSM8K's `<<expr=result>>` markers.
Mistral-7B-SFT emits them in **87.8%** of steps — it was fine-tuned on GSM8K
itself. Qwen emits them in **14.5%** and writes the rest as LaTeX:

```
\[ \text{Miles Micah ran} = 3.5 \times 8 = 28 \]
Next, we know that Amber and Micah together ran \( 8 + 28 = 36 \) miles.
```

Read with markers only, Qwen's local error rate is **0.0027** — computed on a
biased seventh of its steps, and meaningless. The run's annotation gate caught
this before any number was reported:

```
solve rate       0.8000   band (0.55, 0.95)     PASS
annotation rate  0.1450   floor 0.6             FAIL
```

The solve-rate gate passing is what makes the diagnosis unambiguous: the model
*is* following the prompt and solving the problems, it simply does not write
GSM8K's notation.

`car.data.generated.normalise_notation` rewrites LaTeX and unicode maths as
plain arithmetic, and `local_validity(..., notation="any")` falls back to it
when no marker is present.

### Why the extended definition is an extension, not a redefinition

It is applied to **both** corpora, and on Math-Shepherd it is nearly inert:

| corpus | notation | checkable | local error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mistral-7B-SFT | marker | 88.7% | 0.1305 |
| Mistral-7B-SFT | any | 91.4% | 0.1364 |
| Qwen2.5-7B | marker | 14.5% | 0.0027 |
| Qwen2.5-7B | any | **40.7%** | **0.0315** |

Moving the baseline by 0.6 points while moving the new corpus by an order of
magnitude is the evidence that the extension reads notation rather than
changing what counts as an error.

As a check on the implementation, `marker` notation on Math-Shepherd reproduces
the published C1 of **0.6982** exactly.

## Two estimators for C1, and why the published one cannot travel

| estimator | definition | Mistral | Qwen |
|---|---|---|---|
| `C1 all` | inherited / global over **all** steps | 0.7178 | **0.2539** |
| `C1 checkable` | among globally-wrong **checkable** steps, fraction locally valid | 0.7848 | **0.9040** |

`C1 all` is the denominator behind the README's 69.8%. It is biased **down** by
the uncheckable rate: a step with no arithmetic can never enter the numerator
but always sits in the denominator. On Math-Shepherd, where 91% of steps are
checkable, the bias is small. On Qwen, where 73% of wrong-answer steps have no
arithmetic at all, it collapses the estimate to 0.25 and says nothing about
reasoning.

**`C1 checkable` is the estimator to quote across generators.** The two agree
closely when coverage is high, which is why the distinction never surfaced
before.

## What else moved

**C5 — the α floor is generator-dependent, and largely evaporates.**
μ drops 0.3908 → **0.1221**, on the project's existing convention of weighting
the two strata by *solution* accuracy. (The step-weighted rate over the raw
sample is 0.1788; correct solutions run longer, so the step mix is 70/30 where
the solution mix is 80/20. The Mistral figure uses the solution weighting, so
this one does too.)

| α | 0.05 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mistral floor | 35.9% | 32.3% | 23.8% | 13.0% |
| Qwen floor | 7.6% | 2.5% | **none** | **none** |

The Kotte impossibility bound has not weakened; the base risk it applies to
has. On a strong generator, α = 0.20 is attainable with no entry fee at all.
Any claim about the floor must name its generator.

**C2 — corruption is still absorbing, but less so.**

| | Mistral | Qwen |
|---|---|---|
| steps after the first bad step still bad | 95.9% | 66.4% |
| solutions that fully recovered | 0 / 25,971 | 6 / 500 |

"Near-absorbing" was measured on Mistral and does not transfer unqualified.
Recovery is rare but no longer unobserved.

**C4b — the position gradient replicates.** corr(position, local error) =
**+0.866**, against +0.950 on Mistral. Same sign, same conclusion: later steps
are harder, and front-loading is not favoured.

## Limits

- **n = 125.** Qwen's globally-wrong checkable steps, against Mistral's 46,555.
  The Wilson interval [0.840, 0.944] excludes the Mistral value, but C1 is not
  precisely located.
- **59% of Qwen's steps contain no arithmetic.** They are narration — *"First,
  we determine how many miles Micah ran."* C1 is computed on the checkable 41%,
  and if narration steps carry corruption at a different rate the estimate is
  biased. This is not fixable by better extraction; those steps assert nothing
  a calculator could check.
- **A "step" is not model-invariant.** Qwen writes 5.15 steps per solution
  against Mistral's ~3.6, and most of Qwen's are prose. Math-Shepherd's step is
  one calculator operation; Qwen's is one line of explanation. Per-step rates
  across models are rates over different units, and the thesis should say so
  rather than tabulate them as if they were commensurable.
- **The generator is not the one the thesis names.** Llama 3.1 8B is
  licence-gated on Kaggle; the API returns *"User has not consented to terms of
  use"* and Kaggle silently declines to mount the model, so two sessions ended
  at setup. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is the same size class and roughly the same
  GSM8K accuracy, which is the property the argument needs. Re-running on Llama
  needs no code change — `gpu_generator_transfer.py` takes `--model`, and the
  kernel prefers a local mount when one is attached.
- **Global labels are Monte-Carlo, K = 4.** Same estimator as Math-Shepherd's
  hard estimation, so the two are comparable, but a step is labelled `+` if any
  of four rollouts reaches the gold answer — an optimistic estimate of "leads
  to a correct answer", not a proof.
